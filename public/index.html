Leading the people and decisions behind reliable research.

My enterprise career has focused on information quality, governance and analytical systems. My years in the fire service shaped my approach to accountability, team development and decisions under uncertainty.

For a Senior Manager, Customer Research & Insights role, I would combine those foundations with the specialist expertise of research managers and business partners. This portfolio separates what I have done professionally from what I propose and demonstrate in the research lab.

My leadership philosophy

Give people clarity,
then give them room
to do their best work.

The approach I would bring to a research team draws on enterprise governance and years of volunteer leadership.

01

Set a clear direction.

Agree the outcome, identify who owns the decision and make responsibilities explicit. Give specialists the context they need to exercise judgment.

02

Develop people, not just processes.

Coach through real assignments, invite different perspectives and encourage constructive challenge. Make quality a shared responsibility rather than a policing exercise.

03

Stay accountable when the answer is uncertain.

Make evidence limitations visible, escalate concerns and evaluate what happens after decisions are made. Learn openly when an approach does not deliver the intended result.

05 Third-party evidence appraisal

Research judgment beyond
my own analysis.

An independent executive reading of a public Statistics Canada study, illustrating how I would evaluate the usefulness and limits of external research.

Published research · historical baselineExecutive appraisal

Who gambles and who experiences gambling problems in Canada

Michelle Rotermann and Heather Gilmour · Statistics Canada · August 9, 2022 · 2018 CCHS Gambling Rapid Response

24,983Eligible non-proxy respondents aged 15+, in ten provinces
58.8%Overall rapid-response survey response rate
62.2%Past-year participation in Ontario in 2018 (95% CI 60.0–64.4%)

What is credible

The study uses a population survey, weighted estimates and bootstrap uncertainty adjusted for survey design. It provides a useful pre-market-change reference point.

What it cannot establish

The cross-sectional, self-reported observations precede major regulatory and pandemic changes. Nonresponse, underreporting and excluded populations constrain interpretation. The sample is not an OLG customer sample.

How I would use it

Use the historical estimates to frame population-level research questions and compare harmonized measures over time. Do not treat them as current market share, current customer preferences or causal evidence.

What to commission next

Design an accessible, ethically governed adult tracking study with current channel measures, explicit sampling and weighting, protective-tool comprehension and appropriate qualitative follow-up. Review independent contemporary sources before investment.

Executive recommendation

Retain the study as historical context; refresh evidence before making present-day customer decisions. Use it to support player-health research, not promotional targeting.

Read my methodological review and decision boundaries

Population and coverage

Fieldwork ran from July to December 2018. The module covered non-proxy household respondents aged 15 and older in ten provinces; certain populations were excluded from the survey frame. For Ontario, the reported 62.2% estimate describes past-year participation in any covered gambling activity, not engagement with a particular operator. It is not an estimate specifically for adults eligible to use OLG products.

Precision versus applicability

The Ontario 95% interval of 60.0%–64.4% reflects the authors' design-based uncertainty calculation; it does not account for every possible coverage, response or measurement bias. The 58.8% response rate warrants examination of the weighting and nonresponse assumptions. An apparently precise historical estimate can still be unsuitable for a current decision.

Recommended next step

Before comparing results over time, align the age range, target population, question wording, activity definitions, collection mode and weighting. Commission complementary research about accessibility and protective-tool comprehension if those decisions are not addressed by the external study. Report both uncertainty and differences in definitions to the decision owner.
